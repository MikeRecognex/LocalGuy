AMD's latest benchmark study quantifies the real-world impact of on-device AI acceleration on productivity workloads. By running AI-powered project management tasks locally on Ryzen AI processors rather than relying on cloud services, users experienced measurable time savings—up to 18 hours per week for common workflows like scheduling optimization, task prioritization, and resource allocation.

For local LLM practitioners, this study provides concrete evidence that on-device inference delivers practical benefits beyond theoretical advantages. Eliminating cloud API latency, network round-trips, and privacy concerns directly translates to faster task completion and better user experience. The fact that commodity laptop hardware can now provide these gains demonstrates how mature the ecosystem has become—no specialized hardware or complex setup required.

This research also highlights an emerging use case: lightweight domain-specific models running on consumer CPUs and integrated NPUs for business applications. As AMD, Intel, and Qualcomm continue optimizing their processors for AI workloads, the economics of local inference versus cloud APIs become increasingly favorable, especially for organizations prioritizing data privacy or requiring offline-capable systems.
