Modular's MAX framework has demonstrated substantial performance gains over established inference engines, achieving 5.5x faster inference compared to vLLM when running Kimi K2.5 models. This represents a meaningful breakthrough in local inference optimisation, addressing one of the primary bottlenecks for practitioners deploying large models on consumer and edge hardware.

The performance differential highlights the continued evolution of inference engines specifically optimised for local deployment. Rather than relying solely on quantisation strategies, MAX appears to leverage low-level kernel optimisations and better utilisation of available hardware resources to achieve dramatic speedups.

For operators running local inference workloads at scale—whether for RAG systems, batch processing, or real-time applications—this class of performance improvement directly translates to reduced hardware requirements, lower latency responses, and better resource utilisation. The gap between inference framework implementations remains a critical consideration when selecting deployment infrastructure.
