Comparative benchmarking reveals that Qwen 3.5's smallest dense model (3.5B parameters) substantially outperforms the best 9B models from two years prior. This progression illustrates the accelerating efficiency curve in open-source LLM development—newer architectures achieve better performance with fewer parameters, enabling dramatically more efficient local deployment.

For practitioners making hardware choices or model selection decisions, this data point is humbling and encouraging. The implication is that 2024's flagship models are now commoditized into sub-4B variants without meaningful capability loss. This shifts the practical frontier of local deployment: tasks that required 13B or larger models in 2024 can now run on 3.5B efficiently.

The progression also explains why the LocalLLaMA community remains focused on Qwen 3.5—it's not just releasing new models, but fundamentally changing efficiency expectations. This efficiency gain means broader device coverage (older phones, embedded systems, constrained edge devices) while maintaining performance standards that would have required desktop-class hardware just 24 months ago.
