New open-source platforms are emerging to facilitate collaborative development and deployment of local LLMs, moving beyond individual model serving toward integrated ecosystems for teams and organizations. Comparisons between OpenClaw, Eigent, and Claude Cowork highlight the growing maturity of collaborative infrastructure for self-hosted AI development.

For local LLM practitioners working in team environments, these platforms address a gap between single-user tools like Ollama and enterprise solutions, offering features for model management, collaborative fine-tuning, and shared inference infrastructure. The proliferation of such platforms suggests healthy market momentum around local AI infrastructure tooling, even as cloud providers dominate headlines.

Practitioners evaluating collaboration platforms should assess how these tools integrate with existing workflows, support for popular frameworks (llama.cpp, MLX, vLLM), and whether they enable efficient resource sharing across multiple inference workloads without unnecessary overhead.
