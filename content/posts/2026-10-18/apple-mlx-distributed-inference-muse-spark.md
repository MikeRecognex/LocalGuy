Apple's MLX framework continues to mature with new distributed inference capabilities demonstrated on Mac Studio hardware. This research release showcases how MLX enables efficient multi-GPU and multi-CPU coordination on Apple Silicon systems, allowing developers to parallelize inference workloads across available compute resources without sacrificing simplicity.

The Muse Spark implementation on Mac Studio represents a significant step toward making Mac hardware a viable platform for serious local LLM inference at scale. Prior limitations around single-device constraints are being addressed through thoughtful distributed inference patterns, opening Apple's ecosystem for more demanding inference workloads. For developers targeting macOS environments, this demonstrates MLX's continued evolution as a production-ready framework.

For practitioners invested in Apple Silicon infrastructure, whether through Mac Studios, MacBook Pros, or Mac minis, this development validates the hardware choice for local inference. As MLX continues adding features like distributed inference, it competes increasingly effectively with specialized inference engines on NVIDIA and AMD platforms, leveraging the efficiency advantages of Apple's unified memory architecture.
