Power delivery infrastructure often gets overlooked in local LLM discussions, yet it's foundational for edge deployment scenarios. USB PD 3.2 with Advanced Voltage Switching (SPR) support represents a quiet but significant evolution in what's possible for portable and edge AI workloads. Higher power delivery via USB means more capable devices can remain untethered and flexible in deployment.

For practitioners running LLMs on mobile, laptop, or specialized edge hardware, efficient power delivery directly impacts inference throughput and thermal characteristics. Devices that previously required bulky external power supplies can now draw substantial power through USB-C, enabling more compact and flexible deployments. This matters especially for emerging edge AI scenarios: field robotics, remote sensing, on-device research, and consumer applications where power efficiency and form factor are constraints.

As GPU vendors optimize for mobile and power-constrained environments—see recent developments in mobile GPUs and systems-on-a-chip—the infrastructure supporting them becomes equally important. [This hardware evolution](https://charge-test.com/verbatim-mini-gan-charger-140w-review-one-of-the-first-chargers-with-full-usb-pd-3-2-avs-support/) enables the next generation of untethered local LLM inference devices, from high-end mobile phones to specialized edge appliances.
