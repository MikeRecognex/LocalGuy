BibCrit demonstrates the emerging trend of domain-specific language models optimised for local deployment. By grounding an LLM in the ETCBC (Eep Tal Consortium for Biblical Criticism) corpus, this project creates a specialised tool for biblical scholars and textual critics—a community that benefits from local, offline inference without relying on general-purpose API providers.

This approach is highly relevant for practitioners building vertical applications where domain expertise matters more than general intelligence. The model can be run entirely on-device, ensuring data privacy for sensitive scholarly work and enabling researchers to maintain control over their analysis pipeline. It's also a practical blueprint for others wanting to fine-tune open models on proprietary or specialised datasets.

Explore the [BibCrit repository](https://github.com/Jossifresben/BibCrit) to see fine-tuning techniques, corpus integration patterns, and how to adapt the approach for other domain-specific applications. This serves as a template for academic and enterprise users building custom local LLM systems.
