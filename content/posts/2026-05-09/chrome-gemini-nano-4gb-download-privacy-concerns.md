Google has quietly begun downloading Gemini Nano, a 4GB on-device AI model, to Chrome browsers without explicit user consent. Users discovered the installation only after noticing significant storage consumption, sparking concerns about transparency and user autonomy in local AI deployments.

This development highlights a critical tension in the edge inference space: while on-device AI offers privacy benefits over cloud-based alternatives, silent installations without user awareness undermine trust. Several reports indicate that [deleting the model doesn't permanently remove it](https://www.msn.com), as Chrome automatically re-downloads it on subsequent updates, further frustrating users who want control over their local resources.

For local LLM practitioners, this serves as both a cautionary tale and a validation of the open-source alternative approach. Community-driven projects like Ollama and llama.cpp place users in control of what gets installed and when, a principle that many now view as essential for responsible edge AI deployment.
