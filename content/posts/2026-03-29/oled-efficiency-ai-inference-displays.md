While model optimisation typically focuses on compute and memory, the holistic power budget of on-device AI systems encompasses display technology as well. [OLED's emergence as the preferred display standard for AI devices](https://www.chosun.com) reflects recognition that energy efficiency across all system components determines practical viability of local LLM deployment on mobile and edge devices.

OLED displays consume significantly less power than LCD alternatives, particularly when rendering the dark interfaces common in many AI applications. For practitioners building local LLM applications on mobile devices or battery-constrained edge hardware, this seemingly tangential choice becomes material to overall system performance and battery life. The cumulative effect of efficient display rendering amplifies the benefits of model quantisation and inference optimisation.

This trend underscores an important principle for local AI practitioners: end-to-end system efficiency requires optimisation across all layers, not just the model and inference engine. As mobile and edge deployments become more mainstream, attention to power consumption at every component—from display technology to memory hierarchy to compute units—becomes increasingly important for sustainable, practical applications.
