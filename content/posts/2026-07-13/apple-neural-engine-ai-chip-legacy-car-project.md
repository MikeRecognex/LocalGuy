Apple's ambitious but ultimately abandoned self-driving car project has yielded unexpected benefits in the form of advanced AI chip architectures. The neural processing expertise developed for autonomous driving requirements has been repurposed and integrated into Apple's broader chip roadmap, particularly in the neural engines of recent and upcoming processors.

For the local LLM community, this matters because Apple's investments in on-device neural processing directly translate to better hardware capabilities for running models locally on iPhones, iPads, and Macs. The efficiency gains and architectural innovations developed for the car project are now flowing into consumer devices, making local inference on Apple platforms increasingly viable without cloud dependencies.

As [The Verge reports](https://www.theverge.com/2024/1/15/apple-self-driving-car-ai-chips), this shift reflects a broader industry trend toward on-device AI, with Apple's M-series chips increasingly prioritizing neural processing capabilities alongside traditional computing performance.
