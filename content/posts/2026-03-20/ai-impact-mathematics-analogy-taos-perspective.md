Renowned mathematician Terence Tao has offered a [thought-provoking analogy](https://mathstodon.xyz/@tao/116252708577614828) about AI's transformative role in mathematics: just as the automobile fundamentally reshaped cities—changing not just transportation but architecture, social structure, and possibility—AI is reshaping how mathematical research is conducted, what problems are tractable, and which approaches yield discovery.

This perspective has practical implications for local LLM practitioners. If AI is fundamentally changing knowledge work workflows, then local deployments need optimization not just for inference speed, but for the kinds of reasoning patterns that enable sustained intellectual work. Models deployed locally should be tuned for iterative exploration, multi-step reasoning, and the cognitive patterns that emerge when AI becomes a thought partner rather than just a tool.

For researchers and knowledge workers deploying models on-device, this analysis suggests investing in models and optimization techniques that support longer reasoning chains, better context retention, and integration with personal knowledge bases—the infrastructure for AI as a collaborative thinking partner rather than merely a query-response system.
